Online Shopping and Consumer Behaviour in Australia

FIT2179 Data Visualisation 2

This visualisation explores how online shopping in Australia has changed over time, what consumers buy, who drives online spending, and where eCommerce activity is strongest.

1. Market Growth Overview

Australia’s online retail market has grown significantly over the last two decades, with major changes occurring during and after the COVID-19 pandemic. This section introduces the overall market trend and the changing reasons why Australians shop online.

Retail Turnover in Australia

Retail turnover has increased across most categories since 2000. The sharp changes around 2020–2022 show how pandemic-related behaviour accelerated online and retail activity, before the market began stabilising again.

Factors that Drive Online Shopping

Convenience remains the primary reason Australians shop online, but cost-related factors such as discounts and price comparisons became increasingly important in 2023. This suggests that online shopping is no longer only about convenience, but also about managing household budgets.

2. What Australians Buy Online

After examining the overall market, this section explores which product categories attract the highest levels of online spending and which are experiencing the fastest growth.

Online Spend by Category (2025)

Online marketplaces, food and liquor, fashion, and home-related categories are among the largest contributors to online spending. This shows that online shopping is now used for both discretionary and everyday purchases.

Retail Category Spending and annual growth (2024-2025)

The bubble chart compares category size and growth. Some categories with lower total spending show strong growth, while large categories continue to dominate overall online expenditure.

3. Who Shops Online?

Online shopping behaviour differs strongly across generations and shopper types. This section focuses on the people behind online spending, showing how different groups shop, spend, and discover products.

Shopping Behaviour by Generation (2025)

Younger generations show a higher online shopping share compared with older groups, but in-store shopping remains important across all generations.

Online Spending by Generation (2024)

Millennials lead online spending, while older cohorts show stronger growth from a smaller base. This indicates that online shopping adoption is still expanding among older consumers.

Shopper Segments and Spending Contribution

Although Super Shoppers represent only 16% of Australian online shoppers, they account for half of all online spending volume. In contrast, Sometime Shoppers make up the largest share of shoppers but contribute only a small share of spending. This highlights the importance of retaining frequent and high-value online shoppers.

Social Media Product Discovery by Age Group

Social media platforms influence product discovery differently across age groups. Gen Z relies heavily on TikTok and Instagram, while older generations are more likely to use Facebook. This shows why retailers need different digital marketing strategies for different audiences.

4. Where Online Shopping Happens

Online shopping is not evenly distributed across Australia. This section examines geographic differences in spending and growth, before showing which eCommerce sites dominate online traffic.

Online Spending in Australia

New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland record the highest online spending, reflecting their larger populations and stronger retail markets. The map helps users quickly compare the geographic concentration of online spending.

Annual State Growth in Online Shopping

Regional states such as Queensland and Western Australia experienced the strongest growth in online shopping activity, while highly urbanised states such as New South Wales and Victoria recorded negative year-on-year growth. This suggests that post-pandemic online shopping behaviour shifted differently across Australia.

Top eCommerce Sites by Monthly Visits

The eCommerce traffic treemap shows that Australian online retail attention is concentrated among a small group of major platforms. Large retailers such as Kmart, Amazon, Bunnings, Woolworths and JB Hi-Fi receive high monthly traffic, showing the dominance of established brands in Australia’s online shopping landscape.
